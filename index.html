What to listen to in an emergency

1. Turn on any AM/FM radio (car or battery)

92.9 FMKXFG — Riverside County emergency alert station, tower 6.8 mi N (Menifee)
95.1 FMKFRG — Riverside County emergency alert station
640 AMKFI — Riverside County emergency alert station (Los Angeles, 50 kW)
1070 AMKNX — Riverside County emergency alert station (Los Angeles, 50 kW)
980 AMKFWB — Riverside County emergency alert station
600 AMKOGO — San Diego County primary emergency alert station
1360 AMKLSD — San Diego County secondary emergency alert station

2. NOAA Weather Radio (needs a weather-band radio)

162.400KEC62 Mt. Woodson, 35 mi S — strongest nearby; its alert tones are set for San Diego County
162.500WXM66 San Bernardino Mtns, 51 mi N — alert tones for Riverside & San Bernardino
162.400KIG78 Coachella area, 68 mi E — alert tones for Riverside & Imperial
162.450WWG21 Santa Ana — serves Riverside County but listed OUT OF SERVICE by NWS
006065SAME code for Riverside County (San Diego County = 006073)

3. Talk with family & neighbors (no license)

FRS ch 1–22Walkie-talkies. Ch 1–7 & 15–22 up to 2 W (longer range); ch 8–14 only ½ W, usually under ½ mile
CB ch 927.065 MHz — reserved by FCC for emergencies & traveler help
CB ch 1927.185 MHz — busiest channel, highways
MURS151.820 / 151.880 / 151.940 MHz

4. Ham radio (listen: anyone · talk: FCC amateur license)

146.5202 m national simplex calling frequency
446.00070 cm national simplex calling frequency
144.895 +W6CDW Elsinore Peak, tone 156.7 — ARES/RACES-affiliated system, emergency power
445.620 −W6CDW Elsinore Peak, tone 173.8 — RACES / CERT / Menifee Valley ARC nets
147.000 +K6KTA Temecula (Rainbow Ridge), tone 136.5
146.805 −W6GTR Temecula, tone 100.0
